Box & material

Length & width
Inside is measured between the inner faces. Outside is the overall size. Length and width differ by twice the material thickness. With a floor, height differs by one thickness. Lid side height differs by the panel thickness. Flipping this rewrites those numbers so the box stays the same size.

Dividers Dividers are always measured from the inside faces of the box. Name is written on that divider in the 2D drawing. X is the distance from the left inside wall and Y from the front inside wall, in millimeters. These coordinates do not change when length and width switch between inside and outside. From floor is the gap above the floor, and from top is the gap below the top of the box. Either gap can be a distance in millimeters or a percentage of the box height; flipping the unit keeps the same gap. In millimetres, neither one can be more than the box height minus the material thickness. Zero reaches the floor. Finger joint to floor cuts fingers through the floor panel. Turn it off and the divider still sits on the floor, with a straight bottom and no slots in the floor. Tilt leans the divider away from straight up, in degrees. Zero stays perpendicular to the floor. Positive leans to the left as you look from the first point toward the second. A tilted divider is cut longer so it still reaches the same height. Finger joints into the floor and into a square wall stay fingers and follow the lean. A divider that ends squarely on a tilted divider is cut on an angle, and its fingers are perpendicular to that tilted face. A meeting that is slanted in the plan keeps one slot. Two dividers that cross use an overlap joint over the height they share: the one listed first is slotted up from the bottom, and the other is slotted down from the top. A divider that ends on another divider's centerline is finger-jointed into it, the same way it meets an outer wall. Two dividers that end on the same point from opposite sides share those slots, and each tab stops halfway through. The plan draws the bottom face of each divider. A tilted divider shows a dashed line where its top leans, and a divider that ends on it shows a dashed top that runs long or short to meet that line. Finger joint to top is for a closed box. It cuts fingers through the top panel when the divider reaches the top. Turn it off and the divider stops under that panel.

    Kerf test
        
          
          Five corners on one sheet, each 50 mm tall. The kerfs are 0.05 mm apart and centered on the kerf above. Thickness and finger length come from those fields. Cut the sheet and press each pair together. Enter the number on the pair that fits with no gap and no force. NOW is drawn on the pair for the kerf typed in. The outlines are black and the numbers are blue, so the numbers can be engraved instead of cut. The joint is the same finger joint the box uses.
        
      
    
      
    
        
        
      
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Plan
          
            
            This view shows only the bottom face of each divider. Click a divider to select it. Its name appears beside the selected bar. Drag that face sideways. A divider that ends on it stretches so the joint stays put. Drag an end to change its length. The angle stays as it is. Snap to whole mm keeps that drag on a whole millimetre, unless it is close to a wall or another divider. A tilted divider shows a dashed line where its top leans. A divider that ends on it shows a dashed top, longer or shorter, so the joint meets that line. Dragging still moves the bottom face. Looking down on the top, with the front edge at the bottom. Drag the bar under the plan to make this view shorter or taller.
